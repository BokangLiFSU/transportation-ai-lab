Research context

Agencies need evaluation methods that relate technology investments to transportation outcomes. This earlier study developed approaches for connected-vehicle and transportation systems management and operations projects.

Approach

The study evaluates Rapid Incident Scene Clearance, Road Ranger Service Patrol and Smart Work Zone strategies in Florida. Connected-vehicle examples include Gainesville’s Signal Phase and Timing deployment and the I-4 Florida’s Regional Advanced Mobility Elements initiative. Performance criteria distinguish project preparation, planning, design and testing, and operation and maintenance.

The work was conducted through Florida International University for FDOT. It forms part of Angela Kitali’s earlier collaborative research, rather than a new project initiated by this group.

Research outputs

  • Benefit–cost analyses evaluate secondary-crash and delay savings for incident-clearance and service-patrol programs.
  • Connected-vehicle examples show why evaluation criteria must change as projects move from planning to operation.
  • The report assembles performance measures, data requirements and evaluation procedures for agency use.

This Florida International University study forms part of Angela Kitali’s earlier collaborative research for FDOT. It provides a foundation for evaluating transportation technologies through measurable outcomes and project-stage requirements.
