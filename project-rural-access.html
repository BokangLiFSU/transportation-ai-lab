Research context

For residents of small rural settlements, a nearby destination may remain difficult to reach on foot or by bicycle. Highways, missing facilities and discontinuous connections can separate residential clusters from essential activities. This project investigates where those barriers occur and which improvements respond to residents’ needs.

Approach

The study covers nine counties in western Washington, from Lewis County to the Canadian border. Geospatial analysis identifies small, unincorporated residential clusters along state highways and major county roads, then maps barriers to walking and cycling. Research on residents’ needs and perceptions complements the spatial assessment and informs investment prioritization.

The project defines rural residential clusters as small, unincorporated groups of adjacent homes along state highways or major county roads. Spatial analysis identifies barriers within a nine-county study area. Research on residents’ needs and perceptions then connects mapped gaps with specific journeys and local priorities. WSDOT’s planned products combine a geospatial database, prioritization method and reproducible workflow. A complementary agency perspective examines how rural needs enter planning, who is responsible for delivery, and what staffing, data and monitoring arrangements support implementation.

Planned research outputs

  • A geospatial database identifying rural residential clusters and relevant barriers to walking and cycling.
  • A prioritization method that relates mapped infrastructure conditions to residents’ travel needs.
  • A reproducible workflow to support the selection of connectivity and safety improvements.

The study is ongoing, with scheduled completion in February 2027. Angela Kitali and Jeffrey Walters are principal investigators; WSDOT sponsors the work. Grace Young and Brian Wood serve as technical monitors, with David Strich as project coordinator. These relationships connect the research products with agency review and intended use.

Investigators and collaborators

Angela Kitali and Jeffrey Walters are the principal investigators. The Washington State Department of Transportation sponsors the study, and the Washington State Transportation Center publishes the project record.
