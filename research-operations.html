Transportation Data, AI and Emerging Mobility

How sensing, learning and operational models support the evaluation of electric, automated and shared mobility systems.

Methods

Data science and AISystems and operational analysisScenario evaluation

We investigate how transportation systems can be represented, understood and operated as technologies and travel needs evolve. Our transportation AI agenda connects learning and inference with engineering knowledge, network performance and the decisions made by people and institutions.

Transportation records contain information that is difficult to recover through conventional databases alone. Our published work classifies wrong-way-driving crashes from police narratives and uses mixture-density networks to predict incident-clearance duration. Infrastructure research extends to detecting and mapping sidewalks, bicycle facilities and speed-management treatments from imagery and light detection and ranging (LiDAR) data.

Our developing interests concern how uncertainty in travel times and incident duration propagates through a journey. For example, a delay on one leg can determine whether a transfer remains possible. We are interested in combining network structure, service schedules and learned forecasts to examine reliability and access under changing conditions, including the introduction of automated, shared and electric services.
