Earlier research and reports

Published reports from earlier appointments and research collaborations.

These reports document Angela Kitali’s earlier collaborative work on traffic operations, incident management, pedestrian treatments and toll policy. They document substantive contributions to transportation modeling, safety and policy, alongside the related journal publications.

2023

Equity Analysis of WSDOT’s Toll Program: Literature Review

Washington State Transportation Center · WSDOT · WA-RD 931.2

Angela Kitali, Mark E. Hallenbeck and Sam Ricord

The review compares nine U.S. income-based toll programs, examining eligibility thresholds, enrollment procedures, transponder charges and minimum account balances. It distinguishes financial assistance from the practical requirements that can prevent participation. The findings inform WSDOT’s wider toll-equity assessment and emphasize local conditions and continuing community engagement.

2021

Strategies to Identify and Mitigate Secondary Crashes Using Real-Time Traffic Data on Florida’s Turnpike

Florida International University and University of North Florida · FDOT · BDV29-977-48

Priyanka Alluri, Thobias Sando, Angela Kitali, Denis Monyo and Haifeng Wang

Incident records, high-resolution traffic measurements, roadway geometry and rainfall observations cover January 2014 to June 2019 on Florida’s Turnpike Mainline and Extension. The study develops secondary-crash identification and real-time likelihood methods, then examines connected-vehicle applications for mitigation.

2020

Guidelines for Installing Pedestrian Treatments at Midblock Locations

Florida International University · FDOT · BDV29-977-49

Priyanka Alluri, Cecilia Kadeha, Wanyang Wu and Angela Kitali

The study identifies twenty pedestrian-crash hotspots in FDOT District Four and examines traffic, geometry, land use and census characteristics. Decision flowcharts translate the assessment into guidance for selecting midblock crossing locations and treatments. Criteria include existing crossings, pedestrian activity, traffic volumes, speeds and roadway configuration.

Library record and full reportDr. Angela Kitali

2020

Developing Florida-specific Mobility Enhancement Factors (MEFs) and Crash Modification Factors (CMFs) for TSM&O Strategies

Florida International University and University of North Florida · FDOT · BDV29-977-46

Priyanka Alluri, Thobias Sando, Cecilia Kadeha, Henrick Haule, Jimoku Salum, Md Sultan Ali, John Kodi and Angela Kitali

The research evaluates ramp metering, dynamic message signs, service patrols, express lanes, transit signal priority and adaptive signal control. Outcomes include reliability, incident clearance, crash frequency and secondary-crash risk. Results were incorporated into a spreadsheet-based TSM&O Strategies Assessment Tool for estimating mobility and safety benefits.

2018

Evaluation of Incident Response Improvements for Statewide Application: Learning from the New Regional Traffic Management Center in Jacksonville, Florida

University of North Florida and Florida International University · FDOT · BDV34-977-10

T. Sando, P. Alluri, C. Chuan, H. Haule, A. Kitali, R. Lentz and A. Huq

The before-and-after evaluation examines Jacksonville’s traffic-management center, which brought response agencies into one facility in November 2015. Hazard-based models analyze verification and response durations. A separate component identifies secondary crashes through changing incident-impact regions and models risk using a Bayesian random-effects complementary log-log formulation.

Library record and full reportDr. Angela Kitali

2018

Evaluation of Photo Red Light (PRL) Enforcement Program in the City of Miami Beach

Florida International University · City of Miami Beach

Priyanka Alluri, Angela Kitali and Fabio Soto

The city evaluation compares nine camera-treated and nineteen untreated intersections, using 2008–2009 and 2011–2013 crash records around the 2010 installations. Full-Bayes analysis complements simple before-and-after comparisons. Results vary by intersection form and crash type, including lower angle-crash counts alongside increased rear-end crashes at several four-leg sites.

City project presentationDr. Angela Kitali
