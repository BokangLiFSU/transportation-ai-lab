Research agenda

Our safety research links road users, infrastructure and traffic conditions with crash and injury outcomes. Angela Kitali’s work on secondary crashes examines the additional risk following an initial incident. Related studies evaluate traffic signals, service patrols and driver warnings. Recent collaborations extend to automated-vehicle safety, micromobility injuries and access to post-crash care, with statistical and machine-learning models chosen for the outcome and data available.

The longer-term agenda concerns proactive assessment and the evaluation of changing road-user interactions. A warning system, automated vehicle or street redesign may alter behavior and exposure at the same time. We are interested in methods that connect measured responses with safety outcomes and test how those relationships change across locations. Such work requires explicit treatment of uncertainty and the limits of observational comparisons.

Methods

Statistical and Bayesian models, crash and exposure analysis, behavioral observation, and the evaluation of traffic-control and infrastructure interventions.
