Research question

A bus rapid transit system must be evaluated through both the service it provides and the safety conditions along its corridors. These studies examine Dar es Salaam’s first BRT system through operational and crash evidence.

Data and methods

The operational study compares BRT, conventional buses and private automobiles along a 10.2-kilometer section of Morogoro Road. Bayesian hierarchical modeling and hypothesis testing compare travel times and examine sources of variability. A separate safety study uses 2016–2020 crash records, hotspot analysis, crash-narrative text analysis and Bayesian networks.

The operational comparison is cross-sectional because pre-BRT data were unavailable. It therefore compares modes along the study corridor rather than estimating a before-and-after effect. The safety analysis examines the spatial concentration of crashes and associations between recorded crash characteristics and injury severity. Together, the studies distinguish service performance from the safety outcomes of the same transport system.

Published research

  • The operational study compares travel times and their variability among three modes on a 10.2-kilometer section of Morogoro Road.
  • The safety study analyzes 2016–2020 crashes using hotspot analysis, narrative text networks and Bayesian networks to examine crash locations and injury severity.
  • The operational comparison is cross-sectional; it does not estimate a before-and-after effect of introducing the system.

Investigators and collaborators
