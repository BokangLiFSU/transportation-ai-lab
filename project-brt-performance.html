Research question

A bus rapid transit system must be evaluated through both the service it provides and the safety conditions along its corridors. These studies examine Dar es Salaam’s first BRT system through operational and crash evidence.

Data and methods

The operational study compares bus rapid transit (BRT), conventional buses and private automobiles on a 10.2-kilometer section of Morogoro Road in Dar es Salaam. It uses Bayesian hierarchical modeling and hypothesis testing to compare travel times and variability. A separate safety study analyzes 2016–2020 crashes through hotspot analysis, narrative text networks and Bayesian networks.

Because pre-BRT travel-time data were unavailable, the operational comparison is cross-sectional. It compares modes using the same corridor, rather than estimating the effect of introducing BRT. The safety analysis examines crash locations and the relationship between recorded crash characteristics and injury severity.

Published research

  • Predicted mean travel times on the study corridor were approximately 16 minutes for BRT, 28 minutes for conventional buses and 18 minutes for private automobiles.
  • Observed travel-time standard deviations were 3.1, 6.4 and 4.7 minutes, respectively, indicating lower variability for BRT in this sample.
  • Crash hotspots occurred at major intersections and commercial locations. Distraction, collisions with non-motorized road users and disregard of traffic controls were associated with a higher likelihood of severe crashes.

Investigators and collaborators
