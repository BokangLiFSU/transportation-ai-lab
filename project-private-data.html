Project Information

PDaSP transportation project — institutions and research scope

Research Question

Detailed transportation records can reveal individual movements even when their value lies in patterns across many travelers. This collaboration investigates how institutions can share and analyze such records while protecting privacy and preserving their usefulness for transportation research.

Data and Methods

The research combines transportation modeling with differential privacy, secure computation and federated learning. It also addresses how these methods are selected, implemented and audited within a shared data system. Evaluation on transportation datasets examines the relationship between analytical usefulness, privacy guarantees and system performance.

The collaboration targets vehicle speeds, travel times, trajectories, infrastructure observations and trip video. Planned platform functions include recommending privacy techniques and auditing formal protection guarantees. The research evaluates privacy, analytical utility and system efficiency together, including centralized and distributed settings. A transportation task determines which information must be preserved and which disclosures must be prevented.

Research and development objectives

  • Privacy-preserving methods for centralized and distributed transportation-data collaboration.
  • Tools for selecting privacy techniques and auditing the guarantees provided by a data-sharing workflow.
  • Cyberinfrastructure and evaluation procedures that connect privacy methods with transportation applications.

Investigators and Collaborators

The University of Connecticut leads the collaboration with the University of Washington, Illinois Institute of Technology and Iowa State University. Professor Xuegang Ban is the University of Washington principal investigator, and Dr. Angela Kitali is a co-principal investigator.
