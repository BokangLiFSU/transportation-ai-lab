Research agenda

Transportation agencies need more than detections in an image. They need infrastructure records with consistent locations, attributes and evidence of quality. We investigate how imagery, LiDAR and roadway data can describe sidewalks, crossings, bicycle facilities and speed-management treatments. The research combines feature recognition with geometric measurement, spatial alignment and inventory design.

Current work with the University of North Florida connects aerial-image interpretation with pedestrian and bicycle inventories. Washington speed-management research adds corridor screening and treatment classification. Related published work uses natural language processing to classify wrong-way-driving crash narratives and neural networks to estimate incident-clearance duration. These applications require different models, but share a concern with what the input records preserve and what the resulting analysis can establish.

Our longer-term interests include infrastructure change detection, transfer between geographic settings and the integration of complementary sensing sources. For sidewalks, an important question is how local obstructions and changes in usable space can be represented within an existing network. We also study the practical conditions for transportation AI: reliable source retrieval, privacy-preserving collaboration, human review and the skills required to maintain analytical tools. Performance must be assessed against the transportation task, including spatial error, missed features and uncertainty in the information delivered.
