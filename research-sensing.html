Research agenda

Transportation agencies need more than detections in an image. They need infrastructure records with consistent locations, attributes and evidence of quality. We investigate how imagery, LiDAR and roadway data can describe sidewalks, crossings, bicycle facilities and speed-management treatments. The research combines feature recognition with geometric measurement, spatial alignment and inventory design.

Published research provides several starting points. Wrong-way-driving studies classify police narratives and examine the trade-off between short word sequences and interpretable phrases. BRT safety research combines spatial crash analysis, narrative text networks and Bayesian networks to study injury severity. Incident-clearance research uses mixture-density networks to represent variation in clearance times. These are distinct analytical tasks, each requiring a representation suited to the transportation quantity being studied.

Different observations resolve different parts of a transportation problem. A crash narrative may describe an interaction absent from a structured record; imagery can show a crossing, while LiDAR supplies geometric evidence about its setting. We are interested in reconciling these sources when they disagree or omit essential information. Questions concern correspondence to the same location or event, the reliability of inferred attributes, and when expert review is necessary. Evaluation must establish what transportation information is recovered and how its errors affect subsequent analysis.
