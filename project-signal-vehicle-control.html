Research context

Signal timing and vehicle trajectories constrain one another. This study examines their joint optimization at intersections serving motor vehicles, pedestrians and cyclists.

Approach

The model predictive control framework coordinates traffic signals and connected automated vehicle trajectories at different time scales. Multimodal simulations compare concurrent and exclusive pedestrian phases, examining how priority choices affect delay, energy use and surrogate conflicts. The Mcity configuration updates the signal layer every five seconds and the vehicle layer every half-second.

Mcity testing combines one physical automated vehicle with simulated background traffic in a fully automated scenario. Lower automation penetrations of 75%, 50% and 25% are evaluated in simulation. Pedestrian and cyclist interactions are examined in multimodal simulation; broader field testing remains a research task. These experiments test coupled control decisions under specified conditions.

Evaluation and research questions

  • The report evaluates how signal decisions and vehicle trajectories interact across control time scales.
  • Concurrent and exclusive pedestrian phasing create different priorities for vehicle movement and pedestrian service.
  • Simulation and mixed-reality testing provide distinct evidence; larger networks and broader field validation remain extensions.

The study provides a specific foundation for research on coordinated traffic control. Future learning-based approaches can be compared with this optimization framework using feasible trajectories, delay, energy and computation time.
