Research Question

Signal timing and vehicle trajectories constrain one another. This study examines their joint optimization at intersections serving motor vehicles, pedestrians and cyclists.

Data and Methods

The model predictive control framework jointly coordinates traffic signals and vehicle trajectories across two time scales. Multimodal simulations examine concurrent and exclusive pedestrian phases, vehicle delay, energy use and surrogate conflicts. In the Mcity configuration, signal decisions update every five seconds and vehicle control every half-second.

Completed Mcity testing concerns unimodal signal–vehicle coupled control at one intersection, using one physical automated vehicle and simulated background traffic. Pedestrian and cyclist extensions are evaluated in simulation. Scenarios with 75%, 50% and 25% automation penetration are also simulated; these are distinct from the fully automated Mcity configuration.

Evaluation and research questions

  • Concurrent pedestrian phasing provided larger pedestrian time-loss benefits in the evaluated scenarios, while exclusive phasing separated pedestrian and vehicle movements to reduce conflicts.
  • Results depended on priority weights. Some concurrent-phase scenarios increased right-turn conflicts, so the findings do not imply improvement in every safety surrogate.
  • At Mcity, coupled control reduced fuel use, waiting time and queues relative to fixed-time and actuated control. Lead–follow conflict counts depended on the time-to-collision threshold.
