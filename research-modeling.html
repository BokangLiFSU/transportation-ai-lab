Traffic Safety, Risk and Incident Analytics

Understanding how risk emerges across road users, infrastructure and operating conditions, and how interventions can improve transportation safety.

Research Focus

We investigate the processes that produce transportation risk and the evidence needed to evaluate interventions. Research connects traffic conditions, infrastructure, road-user behavior and emergency response across individual, corridor and neighborhood scales. Prediction and treatment evaluation serve different purposes and require corresponding study designs.

Selected Publications

An incident changes traffic conditions as queues form and dissipate, altering both exposure and the identification of secondary crashes. Our Florida research combines incident records, traffic measurements, roadway geometry and rainfall to examine these processes. Related before-and-after studies evaluate pedestrian countdown signals, adaptive signal control and transit signal priority using Bayesian methods.

Research Questions

Dr. Angela Kitali’s incident-management research examines how evolving queues affect secondary-crash identification and risk estimation. Bayesian before-and-after studies evaluate pedestrian countdown signals, adaptive signal control and transit signal priority. More recent work examines injury disparities, automated driving systems and emergency medical access, extending the analysis beyond crash counts.

Longer-term questions concern the transfer of risk models across locations, calibrated estimation of rare events and the effects of incomplete reporting. We also examine how exposure and local operating conditions influence estimates of treatment effectiveness. These questions connect AI-based risk analysis with defensible engineering evaluation.
