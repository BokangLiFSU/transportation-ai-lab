Research agenda

Our safety research examines how crashes develop, how injuries differ across road users, and how an incident affects subsequent traffic risk. A recurring challenge is defining the relevant exposure and operating conditions. Angela Kitali’s doctoral research studied the real-time identification and mitigation of secondary crashes, linking incident records with traffic measurements, roadway geometry and weather.

Earlier studies use Bayesian and penalized regression models to examine secondary-crash likelihood and the influence of incident-impact definitions. Evaluations of pedestrian countdown signals, adaptive signals and service patrols investigate specific safety interventions. More recent collaborations address micromobility injuries, older drivers, automated driving systems and access to post-crash care. The methods distinguish outcome severity, incident duration, spatial accessibility and collision type rather than treating them as one prediction problem.

Future work concerns the use of linked infrastructure, traffic and behavioral observations for safety assessment before severe outcomes accumulate. Research questions include how models perform across sites, how changing exposure affects risk estimates, and how warnings can reach drivers early enough to influence a response. We are interested in evaluations that connect predictive performance with intervention evidence, including uncertainty, infrequent crash classes and differences across populations. The aim is to identify when an analytical result supports a specific engineering action.
