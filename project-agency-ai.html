Research question

Transportation agencies need to decide where AI is useful, what evidence supports its use, and how it fits existing responsibilities. This research direction examines adoption across planning, engineering, operations and infrastructure management.

Data and methods

Research development combines evidence synthesis with the design of practitioner enquiry. Questions concern priority use cases, data readiness, implementation barriers, professional skills and the responsibilities retained by agency staff. The intended analysis compares technical possibilities with the tasks and constraints of transportation practice.

The work builds on published transportation-workforce studies and research planning on expert perspectives. The planned enquiry concerns priority applications, perceived limitations and the institutional conditions needed to move from experimentation to routine practice. Expert responses would inform a structured account of use cases and implementation needs; a completed survey or agency deployment is not claimed.

Research questions and planned work

  • How should agencies distinguish a promising demonstration from evidence sufficient for routine use?
  • Which data, skills and organizational arrangements are needed to maintain an AI application?
  • How should professional judgment and human responsibility be retained in analytical workflows?

Investigators and collaborators

Research planning involves Dr. Jeffrey Walters, Dr. Angela Kitali, Dr. Heather Dillon and Dr. Bokang Li, connecting infrastructure systems, transportation engineering, engineering education and AI applications.
