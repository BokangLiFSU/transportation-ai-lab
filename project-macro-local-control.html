Project Information

C2SMARTER project agenda

Research Question

A regional traffic-control model can specify desirable flows into and out of a district, but local signals must implement those targets. This project studies how the two control levels can remain consistent while serving multimodal traffic.

Data and Methods

The project investigates conversion of perimeter-flow targets into boundary signal schemes, optimization of within-region multimodal signals and consistency between regional and local control. The proposed evaluation uses a VTD simulation testbed and calibrated macroscopic fundamental diagram regions in downtown Seattle.

The center’s progress report documents work on the control conversion and Seattle test regions. The sponsor record lists September 2024–November 2025 as the planned period. The study is exploratory, with evaluation centered on simulation and calibration rather than direct application to operational signal control.

Research objectives

  • Translate regional entry and exit flows into implementable boundary signal decisions.
  • Coordinate multimodal intersection timing with network-level objectives and examine control consistency.
  • Evaluate the proposed integration in simulation and discuss implications with agency and industry partners.

Investigators and Collaborators

Professor Xuegang (Jeff) Ban is principal investigator and Dr. Angela Kitali is co-principal investigator. The C2SMARTER project record lists Seattle DOT and CLR Analytics as partners.
