  • Social and Infrastructure Factors Shaping Road Safety: A Multi-Level Analysis of Crashes in Ohio, Texas, and Washington
    2025 · Full report (PDF)

Research question

Traffic injury is unevenly distributed across road users and communities. This completed study examines how individual characteristics, neighborhood conditions and roadway infrastructure are associated with those differences in Ohio, Texas and Washington.

Data and methods

The study links three scales of analysis. Individual-level models examine injury outcomes among pedestrians, bicyclists and vehicle occupants; census-tract analyses relate crash patterns to neighborhood conditions. Roadway assessments apply Safe System principles in Cleveland, Austin and Seattle. The report’s appendices document the statistical methods and supporting analyses.

The framework separates individual outcomes, neighborhood patterns and roadway conditions, rather than assigning every disparity to a single scale. Recommendations include integrating geometry, traffic volumes, speed limits and social data in place-based assessment. Exposure, crash likelihood and injury severity enter the proposed scoring perspective. The technical appendices document the three analytical components.

Findings and recommendations

  • Individual-level analyses examine injury outcomes among vehicle occupants, pedestrians and bicyclists; census-tract analyses examine social and infrastructure context.
  • Roadway assessments in Cleveland, Austin and Seattle apply Safe System principles to place-specific conditions.
  • Recommendations call for disaggregated data, community participation and benchmarks for monitoring interventions and differences in outcomes.

Investigators and collaborators

The report was prepared by Dr. Angela Kitali, Dr. Panick Kalambay, Meshack Mihayo, Dr. Amanda Sesko, Dr. Emmanuel Kidando, Clement O. Lippu, Subasish Das and Jinli Liu. The AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety sponsored and published the research in October 2025.
