Commercial-vehicle safety alerts

Evaluation of in-cab congestion and sudden-slowdown alerts on Ohio highways.

Research context

Commercial drivers need timely information about queues and abrupt speed changes. This study evaluates in-cab alerts intended to improve their response to these conditions.

Approach

The study evaluates Drivewyze congestion and sudden-slowdown alerts delivered through commercial vehicles’ electronic logging devices. It combines trajectory analysis, before-and-after safety assessment and surveys of drivers, agencies and trucking companies. These sources address different questions: when warnings reach drivers, how the evaluated corridors perform and how users experience the system.

Among 1,171 evaluated trajectories, approximately 82% showed braking before the alert arrived. This finding identifies a delivery-timing problem that an aggregate effectiveness measure would miss. The report recommends reducing alert latency and reviewing the size of warning geofences. Its before-and-after comparisons use 2018–2019 and 2022–2023 data; economic estimates depend on the report’s assumptions and the treatment of congestion-related delay.

Research outputs

  • Trajectory evidence identifies warning latency as a practical constraint on driver response.
  • Recommendations address faster delivery and geofence design, alongside user experience and agency implementation.
  • Safety and mobility estimates are reported for the evaluated Ohio corridors, with assumptions documented in FHWA/OH-2024/25.

The study connects measured driving response with system design. A warning can be relevant to a hazardous location yet arrive too late to influence the initial response. The full report provides the trajectory analysis, surveys, economic assessment and recommendations.
