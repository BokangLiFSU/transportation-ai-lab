Research context

Speed-management treatments are difficult to compare when records do not consistently identify their type, location and roadway setting. This research develops an inventory framework for rural corridors and transitions into towns in Washington. The inventory is intended to support corridor screening and the selection of sites for subsequent evaluation.

Approach

The work investigates available LiDAR, imagery and roadway records, subject to source coverage and access. Physical treatments require geometric analysis, signs and feedback devices require detection and classification, and pavement markings require segmentation. GIS processing links the resulting observations to common locations or roadway segments, with duplicate removal and validation.

Current methodological work includes a reproducible GIS workflow for screening rural-to-town speed transitions on Washington state-route mainlines. It uses official route, legal-speed, population-center and land-use records, preserving included, excluded and review decisions. This screening defines candidate corridors for subsequent infrastructure analysis. The complementary inventory work examines which treatments can be recognized or measured from available LiDAR, imagery and asset records, and how detections should be linked and validated.

Planned research components.

Current methods and intended research products

  • A GIS screening workflow and operator documentation for rural-to-town speed transitions, with decision records retained for review.
  • A treatment taxonomy covering traffic-control devices, markings, gateway features and physical roadway treatments.
  • Inventory-method development addressing source coverage, location, classification and the evidence attached to each record.

The inventory describes where treatments are present and the settings in which they occur. Evaluating their effects will require complementary speed, crash and exposure information; inventory detections alone do not establish treatment effectiveness.

Investigators and collaborators

Angela Kitali leads the research, with Sagar Keshari and Bokang Li at the University of Washington, Tacoma Campus. Collaboration includes Thobias Sando and Soud Mbegele at the University of North Florida, Emmanuel Kidando at Cleveland State University and Ren Moses at Florida A&M University.
