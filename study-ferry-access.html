Community profiles for low-carbon passenger-ferry planning

Neighborhood profiles and the planning of potential low-carbon passenger-ferry services.

Passenger-ferry planning requires evidence about the communities a potential connection would serve. This study uses census-tract socioeconomic and demographic data near terminals and marinas to examine possible low-carbon autonomous electric ferry connections in Puget Sound.

Correlation, principal-component and clustering analyses identify four neighborhood profiles. They distinguish conditions associated with poverty, rural and Native communities, aging populations and transit dependence. The paper illustrates potential connections including Port Orchard–Seattle Pier 52, Tahlequah–Point Defiance and West Seattle–Seattle Pier 52.

The results support comparison of access needs around possible services. Subsequent planning must connect those needs with route demand, terminal access, sailing schedules and operating requirements. The study provides community-level planning evidence; the separate Duck-E prototype addresses navigation and near-field docking.
