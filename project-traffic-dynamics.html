Project Information

National Science Foundation award 2450470

Research Question

Connected and automated vehicles introduce interactions that conventional traffic models may not represent adequately. This research investigates how governing traffic relationships can be learned directly from observations while retaining interpretable and generalizable representations.

Data and Methods

The project develops deep-learning structures for noisy traffic data and a coordinated learning framework for heterogeneous vehicle classes and driving behaviors. It evaluates the resulting models through accuracy, parsimony, interpretability and generalizability.

The research addresses model discovery for traffic prediction, planning, management and control. Learning the governing relationships is distinct from fitting an accurate predictor at one site: a useful representation must remain understandable and applicable beyond the data used to estimate it. The project also supports open research exchange and student participation.

Research Objectives

  • Develop learning methods that account for observation noise in traffic data.
  • Represent interactions among different vehicle classes and driving behaviors.
  • Assess learned traffic models using accuracy, parsimony, interpretability and generalizability.

Investigators and Collaborators

Dr. Xuegang Ban is principal investigator and Dr. Angela Kitali is co-principal investigator on the University of Washington award. The NSF-funded collaborative research runs from May 2025 to April 2028.
