Rural transportation: R-SEAT

R-SEAT supports research, education and exchange across rural mobility, infrastructure and transportation workforce studies. Dr. Angela Kitali serves as Associate Director for Education. The center’s April 16–17, 2026 conference combined technical sessions, technology demonstrations, student posters and workshops on user perceptions. Its earlier active-transportation project is distinct from the separate WSDOT rural residential-clusters study.

Privacy-preserving transportation data: PDaSP

The PDaSP collaboration combines transportation applications with privacy, security and computing-systems research. Its scope includes trajectories, travel times, infrastructure observations and trip video. Planned tools recommend privacy techniques, audit protection guarantees and support centralized or distributed data collaboration. Evaluation considers analytical usefulness and system efficiency alongside privacy.

Rural access and infrastructure: WSDOT and TRAC

Dr. Angela Kitali and Dr. Jeffrey Walters co-lead WSDOT’s rural residential-clusters study. The research combines geospatial identification of barriers with evidence about residents’ travel needs to inform walking and cycling improvements. WSDOT sponsors the work and is the intended user of the database and prioritization method; TRAC publishes the project description.

Road-safety disparities: AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety

The AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety sponsored and published the October 2025 report on social and infrastructure factors in road safety. The collaboration examines disparities at individual, neighborhood and roadway levels across Ohio, Texas and Washington. Its technical report and practitioner summary set out the methods, findings and recommendations.

Mobility, policy and the transportation workforce

Two published studies examine different parts of transportation workforce preparation. Transportation Engineering 2.0 uses open-ended responses from seventy engineers and managers, analyzed through thematic coding. A separate study mines 8,132 U.S. job advertisements and compares advertised skills with O*NET expectations and an industry survey. Together, they examine the relationship between emerging professional needs, interdisciplinary training and recruitment.

Traffic control: University of Washington and C2SMARTER

Research with Professor Xuegang Ban connects signal timing, vehicle trajectories and network-level traffic control. The January 2025 report studies joint signal–vehicle control. A separate C2SMARTER project examines how regional perimeter-flow targets can be implemented through local signals, with Seattle DOT and CLR Analytics identified as partners. The projects address different control scales and have separate evaluation records.
