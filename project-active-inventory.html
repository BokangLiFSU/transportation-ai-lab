Detecting and mapping walking and bicycle facilities to support rural-network analysis.

Research Question

Incomplete facility records make it difficult to assess the continuity of rural walking and cycling networks. Missing or inconsistent information can conceal links between local facilities and major roads. This research investigates how imagery-derived inventories can support the identification of facilities and gaps in network coverage.

Data and Methods

The research compares a pretrained pedestrian-infrastructure classifier with a DeepLabV3+ semantic-segmentation model for aerial imagery in Duval County, Florida. Spatial post-processing and human review convert candidate features into geodatabase records. Validation examines classification, positional alignment and network continuity.

The inventory workflow carries image-derived features through quality review into a standardized File Geodatabase with feature attributes and topology rules. A missed segment can interrupt a mapped route, while a false connection can make an infeasible journey appear possible. Longer-term questions concern combining mobile LiDAR with pedestrian-view observations to measure sidewalk geometry and localized obstructions.

Methods under Development

  • Comparison of transferred image classification and semantic segmentation for pedestrian and bicycle facilities.
  • Spatial records linking facility attributes with review and validation information.
  • Topology checks and updating procedures for examining coverage and network connections.

Investigators and Collaborators

Dr. Angela Kitali collaborates with Dr. Thobias Sando and University of North Florida researchers Soud Mbegele and Elia Magesa Machuche. Dr. Bokang Li contributes infrastructure-information methods at the University of Washington, Tacoma Campus. The collaboration connects image analysis, GIS implementation and transportation interpretation.
