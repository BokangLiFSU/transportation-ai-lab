Research context

Incomplete facility records make it difficult to assess the continuity of rural walking and cycling networks. Missing or inconsistent information can conceal links between local facilities and major roads. This research investigates how imagery-derived inventories can support the identification of facilities and gaps in network coverage.

Approach

The work combines computer vision and GIS to identify pedestrian and bicycle facilities in aerial imagery and represent detections as spatial records. Research tasks include classification, positional alignment and comparison with reference or field information. The approach examines both the recognition of individual features and their suitability for subsequent network analysis.

Work with University of North Florida researchers examines high-resolution aerial imagery, semantic segmentation, post-processing and GIS geodatabase design. Facilities include sidewalks, crossings and bicycle infrastructure. Validation concerns positional alignment, feature continuity and the attributes retained in the inventory. A related research direction examines how mobile LiDAR and selected pedestrian-view observations could describe local sidewalk geometry and obstructions within an existing network, subject to independent reference measurements.

Planned research components.

Planned methods and research products

  • Methods for detecting and classifying sidewalks and bicycle facilities from imagery.
  • A geospatial inventory that records feature attributes together with validation information.
  • Procedures for reviewing, updating and using the inventory to examine coverage and connectivity.

A useful inventory must preserve the distinctions that matter to a network analysis, including whether a facility exists and how it connects to other facilities. Source coverage and validation therefore shape both the inventory and the questions it can support.
